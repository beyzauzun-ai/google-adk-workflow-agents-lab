Logline: In an era of purely functional mass production, a visionary industrial designer battles skepticism and the limitations of industry to transform everyday objects into timeless works of art, forever shaping how America perceives beauty and utility. 

Three-Act Plot Outline:

Act 1: The Unseen Potential
*   Introduction to Walter Dorwin Teague: We meet Walter in the early 1920s, a talented but restless commercial artist/illustrator, feeling unfulfilled by the superficiality of advertising. He observes the burgeoning industrial landscape, filled with utilitarian, often aesthetically unappealing mass-produced goods.
*   The Epiphany: Walter has a profound realization: why can't mass-produced items be both functional and beautiful? He envisions a world where everyday objects are not just tools, but "heirlooms" that enrich people's lives.
*   Initial Struggles & Resistance: He attempts to pitch his radical ideas to manufacturers, facing immediate skepticism from engineers and businessmen who prioritize cost-cutting and efficiency over design. They see aesthetics as an unnecessary expense.
*   A Glimmer of Hope: A small, forward-thinking company (perhaps an early interaction with Eastman Kodak) gives him a chance to redesign a seemingly mundane product or its packaging. Despite limited resources, Walter pours his heart into it, proving that good design can indeed boost sales and consumer appeal.

Act 2: Forging a New Profession
*   Building a Legacy: Walter's initial success leads to more commissions. He establishes his own industrial design consultancy, attracting a small team of like-minded individuals who believe in his vision. He begins to define the nascent field of industrial design.
*   Defining the Craft: He faces ongoing challenges in legitimizing industrial design as a distinct and vital profession, battling against the perception that it's merely "styling" or "decoration." He champions the idea that design is integral to function and user experience.
*   Iconic Creations & Public Recognition: We see him at the height of his creative powers, designing iconic products like the Eastman Kodak Bantam Special camera, Texaco service stations, and elegant Steinway pianos. These designs become symbols of modern American living, demonstrating his philosophy of creating "heirlooms out of mass-produced objects."
*   The World's Fair Triumph: Walter's crowning achievement in this act is his pivotal role in the 1939-40 New York World's Fair, where he designs major exhibits (e.g., the Ford Building). This grand stage solidifies his reputation and introduces the concept of industrial design to millions, making it a household term.

Act 3: The Enduring Legacy
*   The Dean of Design: Now widely recognized as the "Dean of Industrial Design," Walter reflects on his journey. He grapples with the evolving landscape of design, new materials, and the emergence of a younger generation of designers with different approaches.
*   Passing the Torch: He faces the challenge of ensuring his foundational principles endure while adapting to changing times. He mentors young designers, instilling in them the importance of beauty, function, and mass appeal.
*   A Final Masterpiece: Walter undertakes a final, significant project that encapsulates his life's work and philosophy, perhaps a product that becomes a timeless classic, demonstrating the lasting power of thoughtful design.
*   Lasting Impact: The film concludes by showcasing the enduring legacy of Walter Dorwin Teague. Industrial design is now a respected and essential profession, and his vision has transformed everyday objects from mere utilities into cherished parts of American culture, proving that beauty and function can indeed go hand-in-hand for the masses.
